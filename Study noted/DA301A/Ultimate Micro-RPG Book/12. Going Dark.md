# Going Dark
**A GAME OF SPECIAL FORCES AGENTS**

**BY DANIEL H. KWAN AND ANGUS E. MACPHERSON**

**NUMBER OF PLAYERS:** 2–5

**PLAYING TIME:** 30 minutes–1 hour

**COMPLEXITY:** 3

**YOU’LL NEED:** 2d6, pencils, index cards.

**GOAL:** Complete missions, save the world.

**TAGS: Genre:** Modern; **Tone:** Adventurous; **Format:** Traditional; **Content:** Espionage, modern combat, military

In *Going Dark*, players assume the role of Special Forces Agents working to ensure the continued stability of the world. Armed with specialized training, weaponry, and equipment, they are our first line of defense against nefarious forces attempting to seize global control.

## SETUP
The first step is to build your Agent. On an index card note your Agent’s name, background information, and whatever personal details you think are significant.

### ATTRIBUTES
You have four attributes that represent your Agent’s skills. Your Agent is better at some attributes than others. Assign the following values as you see fit: +1, +1, 0, -1.

**Combat:** Hand-to-hand fighting, weapons training

**Observation:** Perception, investigation, understanding

**Technical:** Tradecraft

**Interpersonal:** Social skills, culture

### HARM
Each Agent starts with 3 points of Plate (body armor) and 3 of Vigor (physical health). When you take Harm, remove Plate before Vigor. When your Vigor reaches 0, your Agent is dead.

### GEAR
Each Agent starts with 1 weapon, 1 attachment, and 1 equipment. They may make the same choices if they like.

| ***Weapons*** | ***Characteristics*** |
| --- | --- |
| Assault rifle | Medium-range, automatic |
| Sniper rifle | Long-range |
| Shotgun | Short-range, destructive (use to breach) |
| Submachine gun | Medium-range, compact |
| Light machine gun | Heavy, high-capacity |
| Pistol | Short-range, compact |

| ***Attachments*** | ***Effects*** |
| --- | --- |
| Sight | Increases effective range |
| Suppressor | Quiets your shots to avoid enemy detection |
| Flashlight | Disorients nearby enemies to give other Agents an opportunity to act |
| High-caliber rounds | Allows the weapon to penetrate armor and thin surfaces |

### DESIGNING YOUR AGENCY
On a separate index card, choose an agency name, setting, and specialization. Place this at the center of the gaming space.

**Setting:** Recent Past, Contemporary, Near Future, Future

**Specializations (pick 2):** First Response, Security, Assassination, Covert Operations, Counterterrorism, Surveillance, Urban Warfare, Law Enforcement, Hostage Rescue

### DESIGNING THE MISSION
The game master (GM) rolls 1d6 three times to determine the objective. Alternatively, the players and the GM can choose without rolling.

***Primary Objective***

- **1** Locate, secure, and extract hostages
- **2** Secure intelligence
- **3** Secure and defuse a bomb
- **4** Identify/eliminate a target
- **5** Protect a high-value target
- **6** Conduct reconnaissance

***Location***

- **1** Shopping mall
- **2** Yacht
- **3** Museum or campus
- **4** Military base
- **5** Suburban home
- **6** Office building

***Opposition***

- **1** Rogue intelligence Agent(s)
- **2** Criminal syndicate
- **3** Ambitious corporation(s)
- **4** Private military corporation
- **5** Domestic terrorists
- **6** Cybercriminal

## GAMEPLAY
*Going Dark* gameplay is driven by four Moves; **Engage, Evasion, Apply Training,** and **Aid/Interfere**. To initiate a Move, roll 2d6 and add the appropriate attribute. Once the GM has set a scene or established events, Players describe their character’s reaction and resolve it through moves. 10+ is a complete success, with the character achieving their goal. 7–9 is a conditional success with consequences. 6- is a miss and results in the GM introducing negative consequences.

**Engage:** When you attack a target, roll Combat if you use weaponry, or Interpersonal if you attempt to use social pressure. On a 10+, you successfully Engage the target—they won’t pose a threat anymore. On a 7–9, you expose the target, but the threat persists.

**Evasion:** When you attempt to avoid detection or Harm, roll the attribute that best fits the scenario. On a 10+, you avoid danger—but not for long. On a 7–9, you’re safe, but something is exposed. On a 6-, you’re compromised.

**Apply Training:** When you Apply your Technical Training to bypass an obstacle, roll Technical. On a 10+, you overcome the problem. On a 7–9, you manage to get by but risk being compromised. On a 6-, you fail and suffer negative consequences.

**Aid/Interfere:** When you attempt to Aid or Interfere with a character, roll the attribute that best fits the scenario. On a 7+, they receive +1 or -2 to their next roll. If rolled against a nonplayer character (NPC), on a 7+, you affect their results and create a favorable outcome for the agency. On a 6-, your efforts put you in danger.

## ENDING THE GAME
At the end of every session, it is important that all participants at the table debrief over the events following the mission. The agency must answer four questions during the post-mission debrief. Doing so not only humanizes the characters within your story but also makes them feel more genuine.

Debrief questions:

*Did you accomplish your primary objective?*

*Did you avoid collateral damage? If not, what is the fallout?*

*What happens when the Agents return to their normal lives?*

*What are the repercussions of your actions?*
