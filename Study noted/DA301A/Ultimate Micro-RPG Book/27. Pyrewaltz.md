# Pyrewaltz

Love is Coming Again BY SOUP

**Number of players:** 2

**Playing time:** 1–2 hours

**Complexity:** 4

**You’ll need:** 2d4, 2d6, 2d8, a pen, 2 notecards, 4 paper clips, character sheets in the back of this book.

**Goal:** Be the last one standing.

**Tags: Genre:** Science Fiction; **Tone:** Adventurous; **Format:** GMless; **Content:** Rivalry, combat, romance

## OVERVIEW

*Pyrewaltz* is a game that emulates the hot-blooded, messy rivalries in mecha anime. You start as giant armor-clad robots and trade blows until only one of you is left standing, all the while thinking how, in another life, you could have been friends.

*You are here because your spirit burns. Your blood runs hot enough to scald your veins and drip into the earth, deep enough to reach heaven. Your armor of steel and conviction is too brittle to hold your spirit, and only your fragile body of flesh can find absolution in victory. You see another like you. You recognize their steps, and begin to dance.*

## SETUP

Answer the following questions. Write the answers on the notecards:

- **1.** One adjective to describe the last dream you remember.
- **2.** One adjective to describe what you admire about the other player.
- **3.** One weapon to symbolize your last failure.
- **4.** One adjective to describe the last promise you made.
- **5.** One noun to describe what makes you feel validated.
- **6.** One flower/plant that symbolizes your strength.
- **7.** One noun that describes your ideals.

Your Mech Name is the answer to 1 + the answer to 7.

Finishing move is 2 + 3 : 4 + 5 + 6.

Each player answers three questions alone:

- **1.** What does your faction seek to burn?
- **2.** Who was the Kindler of your spirit’s flame and why?
- **3.** What threatens to extinguish your fire?

Share your answers; make sure both players are comfortable with the material before proceeding.

## GAMEPLAY

You have three phases:

- **1.** **Mech** (d8)
- **2.** **Armor** (d6)
- **3.** **Human** (d4)

By default, the highest number on the die represents your Success zone (8, 6, or 4), while the lowest number represents your Failure zone (1). These are marked by two paper clips that players move back and forth across the character sheet included in this book. When you advance Failure, you move the Failure zone right; when you advance Success, you move the Success zone left. Your success and failure markers can occupy the same zone.

**There are three types of results in** ***Pyrewaltz*:** Success, Failure, and Hesitation. At the beginning of the game you can only succeed by rolling 8 and fail by rolling 1. However, certain results will cause you to Advance your Success and Failure zones and expand your range for these results.

Your Success zone is tracked by a paper clip you start at the 8 position on the right side of your character sheet. Likewise, you start a different color paper clip at the 1 position on the left of your sheet. A player piloting a Mech with their Success and Failure ranges advanced one space each will succeed on 7-8 and fail on 1-2.

Results not in your Success or Failure zone produce Hesitation. Based on how the results of each Dance Partner’s roll match up, you will have to answer different prompts and advance your Success and Failure zones.

Both players start as Mechs. You are Dance Partners, and you have history.

Roll your Mech dice (d8) until one player rolls a higher number. That player describes where the first Encounter takes place.

Every round afterward, roll your dice and resolve.

### RESOLVING ROLLS

**Success versus Failure:** The player who rolled Failure advances Failure by 1. The player who rolled Success advances Success by 1 and answers the following prompt:

You take advantage of what you know about your Dance Partner to push them where you want them. Describe what happens.

Choose whether to:

- Gloat
- Preach
- Grunt

Your Dance Partner may respond with a quip, then move on.

**Success versus Hesitation:** The player who rolled Success advances Success by 1, and answers the prompt below:

You gain the upper hand; describe how.

**Hesitation versus Failure:** The player who rolled Failure advances Failure by 1. The player who rolled Hesitation answers this prompt:

Your Dance Partner makes a mistake; describe how.

**Ties:** If players both roll Success, the player with the smaller die size wins the tie, counting it as a Success versus Hesitation. Otherwise, both players advance Success and Failure zones by 1.

### TERMS

**Tension:** Once your Hesitation zone disappears, you enter Tension. When you enter Tension, narrate a scene depending on your phase.

- **Mech:** Recount the last time you reached your breaking point.
- **Armor:** Recount what secrets you still keep.
- **Human:** Recount why you can never forgive your Dance Partner.

**Phaseshift:** A player who phaseshifts sets their Success and Failure zones to their default positions for their next phase, then describes when and where your next encounter takes place.

If you have no Failure zones remaining after resolving a roll, your Dance Partner must phaseshift.

If you have no Success zones remaining after resolving a roll, you must phaseshift.

**Finishing Move:** When your Dance Partner phaseshifts, strike a pose and read your current finishing move:

- **Mech:** 4 + 5 + 6
- **Armor:** 3 : 4 + 5 + 6
- **Human:** 2 + 3 : 4 + 5 + 6

Phaseshifting represents the destruction of one of the Dance Partner’s equipment. Both partners begin the game in Mechs, after the first shift one Mech is destroyed, leaving the combatant in weaker armor. This continues until one combatant is forced to shift out of their own body in the passionate throws of battle.

## ENDING THE GAME

When one or both players must phaseshift but have no phase to shift into, that player is defeated and the game is over.

Narrate an epilogue based on the results. Players shift from Mechs to armored suits, to their unprotected human bodies. Once you are forced to shift out of your human form, you die.

**If you did not win the Last Encounter, answer:**

**1.** Your spirit has been extinguished; how do you carry on?

**If you won the Second Encounter, answer:**

**2.** You exceeded your Kindler’s expectations. Do they still recognize your spirit?

**If you won the First Encounter, answer a, otherwise answer b:**

**a.** Your faction advanced their goals; what did they lose as collateral?

**b.** Your faction sustained heavy losses; what did they manage to salvage?
