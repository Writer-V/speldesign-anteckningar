# Hero Dog Saves Town
Inside the Writers’ Room of Your Favorite Canine Adventure Show

By Alex Roberts

**Number of players:** 3–10

**Playing time:** 15–30 minutes

**Complexity:** 1

**You’ll need:** Large piece of paper, chalkboard, or whiteboard to write down notes on your episode story pitch, writing utensils.

**Goal:** To create a great story for a TV show about a dog—approved by the dog.

**Genre:** Animals

**Tone:** Funny

**Format:** GMless

**Content:** Work stress, wholesome TV, acting like a dog

![[umrpg-f0051-02.jpg]]

**Safety note:** Dogs like nuzzling humans, and many humans like to pet dogs; make sure you and the other players are comfortable with those contacts before initiating them.

![[umrpg-f0052-01.jpg]]

## SETUP
You and the other players are writers for a TV show about a heroic dog. All of you agree that:

* The dog is not magical.

* The dog cannot use human speech.

* The dog does not have opposable thumbs.

* The dog is a dog.

But the dog always saves the day.

**Here’s the twist:** The dog is the star of the show but also its director and lead writer. Pick one player to be the dog. If more than one player wants to be the dog in the first round, pick the player who barks the loudest. The rest of you are human writers.

It’s time to pitch the next episode!

## GAMEPLAY
The HUMAN WRITERS will pitch potential answers to the following questions.

Only the DOG can accept or reject an answer.

The DOG can communicate approval or disapproval in all the ways dogs usually do.

<u>Here are the questions the writers must answer:</u>

**WHERE** is the dog in this episode?

**WHO** is the friend (or friends) the dog makes?

**WHAT** is the problem they are facing?

**HOW** does the dog try to help?

**WHO** tries to get in the dog’s way?

**HOW** does the dog outwit them?

**WHAT** is the last shot of the dog the audience sees before the credits roll?

These questions can be answered in any order. (To challenge yourself, decide on an answer for the final question first!) Write your dog-approved answers down somewhere everyone can see them. Bullet points and drawings are fine.

## ENDING THE GAME
When all the questions have dog-approved answers, pitch the complete episode to the dog. When you are done, the dog will fire one of the human writers. This decision is reached by inscrutable dog logic, fundamentally unknowable to the human writers. The person who is fired gets to be the dog at the next session.
