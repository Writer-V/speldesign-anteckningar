# Van Gogh’s Ear

The Game of Inadvisable Gifts

*By Jenn Ellis and Keith Baker*

![[umrpg-f0089-01.jpg]]

**Number of players:** 2–5

**Playing time:** Under an hour

**Complexity:** 1

**You’ll need:** Standard deck of playing cards with jokers removed.

**Goal:** Give a gift to a fellow player that is both considerate for them and tragic for you.

**Tags:** Genre: Comedy | Tone: Funny | Format: GMless | Content: Cruel dramatic irony, cringingly terrible choices

What’s the worst gift you can give to someone you care about? In *Van Gogh’s Ear*, players craft competing tales of tragedy driven by thoughtful—and terrible—gifts, like Van Gogh’s ear.

### CREATE A CHARACTER

Next you should create a character (see chart on previous page). Are you an impoverished painter? A wealthy socialite who loves dogs more than life itself? Define a **Role**, a **Defining Trait**, a thing that you **Love**, and a **Prized Possession**. You can make up these details, or you can draw playing cards and use the following table for inspiration. Once you’ve come up with the basic details, name your character. Work with the players to your left and your right to finalize your details and, critically, to **define your connection** to each of them. Are you friends? Relatives? Coworkers? Lovers? Why will you be giving each of these people tragic and wonderful gifts?

### SET THE SCENE

Once all characters have been created, take a moment to establish the story. Each player should introduce their character, providing as much or as little detail as they choose and mentioning their relationships with the other characters. Together, decide if the gift-giving takes place at a specific event (if so, where?) or whether it will occur over a period of time. You can use this table to inspire you.

## Gameplay

The player who’s most recently given someone a gift in real life begins. On your turn, look to the person to your right and describe the gift you’re giving them. A few things to consider…

**What does your character have?**

*What sort of things would your character have access to, based on your role and the setting?*

**What does the other character want?**

*You know what they love and prize. What’s the most thoughtful and meaningful gift you could give them?*

**What does the gift cost you?**

*What is the tragic sacrifice your character has to make to acquire or create this gift?*

The goal is to come up with a gift that is both wonderfully considerate but that also requires a tragic sacrifice on the part of your character. For extra tragedy, see if you can find a way to ruin your Prized Possession or the gifts others have given you in the process. If you prize your music collection and you’ve been given the only copy of a special recording, it’s a perfect time to give away the last gramophone in Paris! Regifting is also an option, but is there a way to make it even more of a sacrifice for you than it was for the person who originally gave it to you?

Once you’ve presented your gift, play passes to the left; the next player gives a gift to the person to *their* left. This continues until each player has taken one turn. Then play reverses, with each character giving a gift to the player to their right.

## Ending the Game

Once every character has given and received two gifts, take a moment to reflect on the final situation:

*Did anyone actually come out ahead? Despite receiving and giving terrible gifts, are they somehow better off? Who made the most significant sacrifice? Will anyone actually enjoy their gifts?*
