# Truth in the Mirage

A Game about the Evolution of Stories

By Amr Ammourazz

![[umrpg-f0085-01.jpg]]

NUMBER OF PLAYERS: 2–6 • PLAYING TIME: 1–2 hours • COMPLEXITY: 3 • YOU’LL NEED: Pad of paper, pen or pencil. • GOAL: To discover the hidden heart of a story. • TAGS: Genre: Fantasy, Tone: Serious, Format: GMless, Content: Traditional storytelling, analysis, reinterpretation

*The Desert is eternal; from before its planet even exists until its atoms are scattered to every corner of the universe, the Desert remains, constant. Travelers come and go, and while the Desert forgets the details, lost forever to the sands of time, the message of the story is carried on to eternity, conveyed in the one way the Desert knows how: The Mirage.*

## SETUP

Select a Reader to guide the group through play. Once they have been selected, they should read aloud the intro narration above, and then continue reading the instructions and narration as it appears in the text.

### GATEWAY TO THE DESERT

*You are about to undertake your Journey through the Desert. The Desert is different to each player. To one, it might be a jungle or a void in space where the Desert was or will be. Or it might be The Burning Sands rushing to greet you. Each person has their own Desert to cross, often separated from one another by space, time, or both. And yet, it is always the same Desert.*

You will now collectively create a single character by going through the following series of prompts. One by one, go around the table and have one person read and complete the first prompt; then have the next person read and complete the second prompt; and so on. Repeat this for every prompt in the list until the group has created a character. Do not name your character.

### PROMPTS

- I stand at the Gateway to the Desert. I look out at the Desert and I see…
- I am here because…
- I must travel through the Desert because…
- I know that my Journey will…
- I don’t know it, but my Journey will…
- I am… (don’t name the character; instead give them a defining characteristic)

## GAMEPLAY

Now that the protagonist of the story is created, continue going around the table and have everyone work together to tell the full story of their Journey through the Desert. Each of you should draw on and include narration from the other players, as well as all the details you established in the first set of prompts. The following prompts can provide structure and guidance, but feel free to deviate from them or repeat ones as desired. Do not be afraid to spend time filling in details and fleshing out the story and character, but if you find yourself talking uninterrupted for extended periods of time, invite the other players to weigh in with suggestions and to finish off your thoughts for you.

### PROMPTS

- When I first set out into the Desert…
- On my path I found…
- When I went off my path…
- Something changed when I…
- The most remarkable moment was…
- My Journey ended with…

### THE HEART OF A STORY

With your story complete, go around the table one more time, and have each person explain the significance of the story to them. They should then distill it down into a one-sentence summary, a moral or guiding theme from the story. To you, this is the story’s Heart. The Hearts can have similar themes, but should be distinct from one another.

### THE SANDS’ LAUGHTER SWALLOWS TIME

*Like the Desert’s shifting sands and changing landscapes, the details of a story evolve with every retelling; as the desert perseveres through time, so too does the Heart of a story.*

As a group, take turns retelling parts of the story of the original journey. As you do this, alter, add, or remove elements of the story in order to better highlight the Heart you selected. The core of the story should stay the same, but feel free to change whatever details you’d like to emphasize what you see as the core theme. You can go back to the prompts for inspiration on the types of details you might add to the story.

The following guidelines can help guide your choice of what details to include or emphasize:

- Is this detail fun to keep in? Does it support my Heart?
- Does being more specific here heighten the impact? What about being vague?
- How can I embellish and exaggerate without betraying the integrity of the story?
- How can I rearrange details to make them all shine?

If you want a longer game, loop back up to “The Heart of a Story,” with each player choosing new hearts for the next retelling. Use the already altered story as your base. You can keep doing this as many or as few times as you wish, further altering and shifting the story with each iteration, before progressing to the final section.

## ENDING THE GAME

*You are all one traveler Journeying through the Desert. You must make it to the other side. As you crest another dune, expecting nothing but more sand to greet you, you see an image on the horizon. A snapshot of a past journey. A Mirage.*

Work together to create a single scene that captures the essence of the story. This can be a specific scene that occurred in your narration or a thematic interpretation of the story.

Start by painting in broad strokes to convey the themes behind the story, and add details from there. Use the following prompts for guidance as needed:

- What populates the landscape?
- What colors make up the scene?
- Are there textures or smells that stand out?
- Which pieces are more focused?
- Which pieces are less clear?
- What feeling does it inspire?

Once that’s done, have each person add one final detail to the illusion based on your Heart. It can be as small or large as you want, but it should leave a lasting impression on the scene.

*As you rest atop the dune, the Mirage fades away. It was there for but a moment, but a moment was enough to glimpse the past, to see a long-lost journey, and to understand its Heart.*
