# A Machine Larger Than You
A GAME ABOUT RESISTING FROM WITHIN

**BY BRANDON O’BRIEN**

**NUMBER OF PLAYERS:** 3–6

**PLAYING TIME:** 1–2 hours

**COMPLEXITY:** 3

**YOU’LL NEED:** 6d6 and 1d20 per player, 1d20 for Fate.

**GOAL:** Struggle to end an insidious conspiracy against your troops.

**TAGS:** Genre: Modern Tone: Serious Format: Traditional Content: Oppression, tragedy, hope

You are a commissioned officer in a vast, wide-reaching military apparatus. You and several of your colleagues have discovered a dark conspiracy that threatens the freedom and safety of your stationed area—and worse. Struggle to maintain Hope and challenge the powers that be before the Gears of the system consume you and those you love.

## SETUP
Each officer has actions that they may have proficiency in:

**ATHLETICS:** Running, lifting, jumping.

**INSTINCT:** Awareness, reflexes, smarts.

**INTEL:** Knowledge gained from informants, docs, and other agents.

**OBSERVE:** Read people, spot danger, gain intel firsthand.

**ENGINEER:** Building or repairing tools and tech.

**STEALTH:** Disguise, sleight-of-hand, and remaining hidden.

**SKIRMISH:** Close-quarters combat, armed or unarmed.

**SHOOT:** Combat with long-range weapons.

**COMMAND:** Order and organize others, maintain morale.

**DIALOGUE:** Socialize, comfort, and persuade.

**THREATEN:** Scare, compel, intimidate.

Determine what staff position you hold in this military.

Each position brings with it actions that you are particularly skilled at:

**PERSONNEL OFFICER:** Administrative talent (Command, Dialogue, Threaten).

**INTELLIGENCE OFFICER:** Adept at intel-gathering (Intel, Threaten, Engineer).

**OPERATIONS OFFICER:** Talented organizer (Observe, Intel, Instinct).

**COMMUNICATIONS OFFICER:** Adept at comms (Engineer, Dialogue, Instinct).

**TRAINING OFFICER:** Education maestro (Threaten, Command, Observe).

**ENGINEERING OFFICER:** Adept at tech (Engineer, Instinct, Observe).

Circle one action to determine that you are Unmatched at it; whenever you Challenge someone, a tie becomes a win for you. Underline one action to determine that you are Talented at it; whenever you Challenge someone, a loss becomes a tie. Write down two other actions that you don’t already have. You are All Right at the three actions that are not circled or underlined, which have no benefits attached to them. You are Bad at every other action; whenever a Challenge involves them, you must win one more round in that Challenge to succeed.

Write the names of three Bonds you have with others—your family, lover, close friends, or others—and describe your relationship in fewer than five words. For each of them, place 1d6 with the 6 faceup. These are the Gears of Terror—every moment they feel worried about their world and their place in it, this Gear turns, ticking down one number. For each of them, place another 1d6 with the 5 faceup. These are the Gears of Threat—every time someone threatens to harm one of your Bonds, complicate their life, or sever your relationship to them, this Gear turns.

If either Gear completely turns past 1 back to 6, they will suffer terrible harm, flee your city, or even be taken from you entirely. Write one thing each of them enjoys doing, having, or experiencing. You can keep them calm, and restore a small portion of your Hope, by spending some of your little free time with them and sharing the things they enjoy.

Take a d20 and place the 7 faceup. This represents your Hope. Whenever the Gears linked to your Bonds have turned to 0, or you are made to do something terrible that you cannot bear, you lose one Hope. You gain Hope by spending time with your Bonds (which also ticks their Gears back up one number), or by successfully standing your ground in a conflict or defending your ideals.

Take another d20 and place the 8 faceup in the center of the table. Add 2 for each officer present. This represents the Gears of Tyranny. Whenever those above you succeed in doing terrible and unforgivable things without consequence, this Gear turns. You may unturn it by foiling their plans and revealing their schemes.

Whenever you issue a Challenge—whenever you would otherwise roll in another game—you instead play rock paper scissors. Your GM determines a number of rounds needed to win to succeed, which cannot be less than three. If you win all rounds, you succeed. If you lose one round, you succeed at a cost. If you lose two rounds, you lose.

Unless you are Unmatched at an action, whenever you tie, you must play another round. If, after replaying a tie, you succeed in your Challenge, you Flourish: You are beyond successful at your task, a new opportunity arises, or your enemy considers backing down.

## GAMEPLAY
In the first scene, play opens with the GM describing a mundane day at work for all officers. During that scene, officers will go about their workday as normal, mingling, conducting their regular duties, and so on. During the course of this, the GM will disclose some minor or major evidence of wrongdoing: the disappearance of a fellow soldier gone unreported, alleged improprieties taking place in a settlement nearby, talk of embezzled funds or stolen heritage property, or worse. This scene readies the Gears of Tyranny to turn, and begins play.

In subsequent scenes, a player establishes something they want to get or do. The GM will set the scene and negotiate the conflict that player must overcome for the sake of that want. At any point during play, you can use your actions if the GM permits it and it is suitable to your cause.

Standing against the forces of your institution—your superiors, your duties, and the consequences of them—will always affect the Gears of the machine. If, for instance, you stand your ground in a confrontation with a superior officer but lose the resulting Challenge, or make a decision that may upset a superior’s goals, your moment of weakness may inspire them to threaten someone you love or aim to protect, and the Gears of Threat will turn, with the possible loss of that loved one. If the Gears of Tyranny reach their end at 20, the game ends with all officers being broken.

Play may also end with a positive result—if the officers can manage to foil the conspiracy they have witnessed before the Gears of Tyranny are fully turned—the game ends with the GM declaring that, even though they have defeated the rise of corruption and oppression for now, it may still loom within their ranks, and while they are still at least a little hopeful for the future, they have been undeniably shaken by what they have witnessed.

Whenever an officer is broken, the things they have seen have disturbed them to their core, and they cannot recover from them. Being broken means different things in different circumstances; at times, it means being taken away to suffer silently in a prisoner’s camp or an institution, while in other cases it may mean being utterly brainwashed by the system, or just too compromised to step out of line any longer.

## ENDING THE GAME
At the end of play, the GM dictates to all officers whether the Gears of Tyranny spin on or are jammed by your actions, and the story of your duty ends.
